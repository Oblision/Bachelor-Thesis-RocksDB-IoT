\addchap{Appendix A: Curated Result Tables}

The tables below aggregate the claim-bearing core-evaluation runs, one row per cell; the 32/32 backend-size contrast is tabulated separately in Appendix~E.6.
A cell is one combination of campaign, cap, pool, and workload; reps is the number of runs, OK the completions, rc the modal failure code of the non-completing runs (Section~\ref{sec:bg-metrics}), tps the median throughput of completing runs in million tuples per second, p99 the median 99th-percentile latency, and anon the median peak anonymous memory.
The tables are generated by \texttt{tables/gen\_tables.py} from the per-cell TSVs in the repository; 2{,}092 runs in 260 cells across 14 campaigns.

This appendix is a curated view, not the full raw dump.
The complete evaluation ran 46 campaigns.
The tables here keep the campaigns that carry a headline claim, a pre-registered falsification, or a diagnostic figure.
They drop the redundant exploratory, smoke, and shakedown campaigns, the sanitizer-instrumented diagnostic builds, and the two \textbf{DEMOTED} arms (the lever campaign S and the pool-derived-threshold campaign V) on which no headline claim in Chapter~\ref{cha:evaluation} rests under its original interpretation.
The dropped campaigns remain in the versioned repository for full reproducibility.

Each campaign carries a note stating its configuration provenance and validity.
This matters because of the configuration finding of Section~\ref{sec:rq3}: every campaign that ran the engine-default 4096-byte buffers kept all window state outside the buffer pool, so its data describes the unbounded (heap) architecture and carries no evidence about pooling --- regardless of what the campaign was originally designed to test.
Campaign S is the worked example of this trap: one wrong binary default silently voided a whole campaign's intended question.

\addchap{Appendix C: Negative-Results Ledger}

Every mechanism or explanation below was tested and did not hold.
Each entry carries its one-line evidence.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bucket-directory shrink (one-liner):} two-thirds of maps size their directory on uncovered paths; no completion effect.
  \item \textbf{Aggressive / lazy spill thresholds:} no conversion of failing cells in the threshold-sensitivity arm.
  \item \textbf{Soft threshold derived from the pool:} 0/8 everywhere; cap-derived rule confirmed (8/8 vs 3/8 at the decisive cell).
  \item \textbf{Source admission bound (max\_inflight 64 $\to$ 16):} no change.
  \item \textbf{Task queue bound (1000 $\to$ 8):} no change.
  \item \textbf{Allocation backpressure as a fix:} converted nothing; 10\% throughput cost near the edge; retained as diagnostics only.
  \item \textbf{Levers without pooling (entire first campaign):} mechanically correct, defended an empty pool; conclusion retracted and re-attributed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{E.7\quad Drift control and drain mechanism}
Because the two arms ran in separate sessions, a same-session control re-ran the 512\,MB pooled grid with both configurations interleaved on one freshly formatted card ($n = 80$ each).
It separates session drift from the configuration effect and bounds the absolute 512\,MB claim (Section~\ref{sec:rb32}).

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|l|r|r|r|}
\hline
\textbf{Comparison (512\,MB, $n = 80$)} & \textbf{A ok} & \textbf{B ok} & \textbf{McNemar $p$} \\
\hline
drift: old 16/8 (A) vs.\ fresh 16/8 (B)  & 28\% & 32\% & $0.29$ \\
\hline
in-session: 16/8 (A) vs.\ 32/32 (B)      & 32\% & 40\% & $0.21$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
On cells both configurations complete, the footprint barely drifts between sessions (median peak anonymous memory $-0.3\%$, peak total $0\%$); only throughput drifts ($-49\%$).
The drift-free in-session contrast is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rb32-insession}, and the anonymous-memory trace of one completing run of each backend size in Figure~\ref{fig:rb32-trace}.

The pre-registered drain mechanism (H3) is the bytes the backend writes to the device.
On W\_ pairs where both configurations complete, the median device write of 32/32 relative to 16/8 is $1.60\times$ at 512\,MB, $0.89\times$ at 1024\,MB ($p < 0.001$), and $1.00\times$ at 2048\,MB.
Only the 1024\,MB cap shows the predicted reduction, and at about 11\% rather than the third the component benchmark suggested.
